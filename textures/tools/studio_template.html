PMR Texture Studio

Pick one or more patterns, set ink and paper colors to match any color scheme, fade them across the page, and copy the CSS. The patterns are alpha masks, so one file serves every color scheme.

Colors

Rolls a new ink and paper at random, always with enough contrast to read.

Texture layers

Layers stack in order, so layer 2 sits on top of layer 1. Two large patterns of similar scale can moire against each other: fade one across the other, or pair a large pattern with a fine one (the procedural pile and grain).

Preview

Sample cardBody text should stay easy to read over the texture. Raise or lower the ink strength until it does.

A fade runs across the whole element the texture sits on: the full page height for a page background, or just a hero band if you put the texture inside one.

Credits

    
The patent patterns come from US design patents: six carpet patents by Eugene A. Crowe of Boston and two by Edwin Fisher of Boston (all 1882-1884, expired more than a century ago), and US Design Patent No. 268,339, the Tioga Comp III tire tread (Syoji Inae and Eiji Fukuchi, assigned to Mitsuboshi Belting Ltd., granted 1983, expired 1997). Drawing scans are US Patent Office drawings via Google Patents.

    
    
    
    Patterns marked Adapted are not the patent's own drawing: the drawing was redrawn with an AI image model (Google Gemini) and its edges repaired so it tiles. Only patterns marked Patent drawing are the original drawing. Patterns marked Procedural are generated noise, not drawn from any patent, and need no credit.
